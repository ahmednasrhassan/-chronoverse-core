## Safety boundary: durable legacy exclusion is required for a guarantee

Sanity `publishedAt` is editable. For an unseen old publication, changing that date into the eligible interval makes its current metadata indistinguishable from a pre-cutover draft first published after cutover. Neither `_createdAt` nor `_updatedAt` proves original publication. The empty sequence-1 manifest has no historical article IDs, and the publication journal has no immutable first-publication record for unseen legacy articles. Keeping observed article identity/date/revision protections cannot reconstruct that missing history.

**A durable namespace-qualified exclusion of all 72 genuine legacy IDs, or trustworthy immutable original-publication evidence, is needed to guarantee that these older publications cannot enter after a date edit.** The actual 72 IDs/history were not supplied locally. This change does not invent, seed or implement an empty exclusion inventory and does not claim that date filtering provides the stronger guarantee. A characterization test demonstrates that an unseen legacy date edit currently qualifies; this is an explicit release/activation blocker, not an approved policy relaxation.

Before any production discovery invocation or activation, independently review a complete trusted as-of-cutover legacy baseline and implement an enforced, durable exclusion (including date edits, republishing, namespace binding, missing/incomplete baseline fail-closed checks, and recovery preservation), or adopt trustworthy immutable first-publication metadata. Do not obtain the baseline by simply treating every pre-cutover-created document as legacy: that would reintroduce this bug for old drafts. A future current-date query is not proof of historical publication after a date rewrite. Do not reset CONTROL, DISCOVERY, publication tombstones, article/campaign/recipient claims or content anti-rollback state to bypass this blocker.
